# A1: Programming for Artificial Intelligence Group Assessment Scenario

## Scenario

The university is exploring ways to improve how students access and use campus learning resources. At present, bookings for study rooms, computer labs, specialist equipment, academic support sessions, and group-work spaces are managed separately across different departments. As a result, staff find it difficult to monitor resource demand, identify underused facilities, prevent booking clashes, and understand how students are using available learning spaces.

The Academic Support and Learning Resources Team has therefore asked your development team to design and implement a small prototype system. The system should:

1. Collect and store data according to your requirement analysis, such as student or staff ID, resource type, resource name, booking date, booking time, booking status, department, course, and reason for booking.
2. Provide simple analytics, such as identifying the most frequently booked resources, busiest days and times, cancelled bookings, underused resources, or booking trends over time.
3. Allow authorised users to create, read, update, and delete booking records.

The prototype does not need to be production-ready. However, it should demonstrate how such a system could be developed in Python, with a relational database backend, and include simple data analysis and visualisation features.

## Stakeholder Interviews

As part of the project, your group will be expected to conduct a mock interview with stakeholders. Your tutors will roleplay these stakeholders during the assessment. The interviews are designed to help you clarify requirements and demonstrate that you can translate stakeholder needs into practical software features.

You may meet two main stakeholders:

### 1. Learning Resources Manager

This stakeholder is concerned with efficient use of university learning facilities.

They are likely to ask for features such as:

- reports showing room and equipment demand;
- identification of underused or overbooked resources;
- booking conflict detection;
- summaries to support future planning;
- clear reports for non-technical staff.

They will emphasise usability, reliability, and accurate reporting.

### 2. Student Services Representative

This stakeholder is concerned with fair and accessible student use of learning resources.

They are likely to ask for features such as:

- whether students from different courses can access resources fairly;
- whether students can easily find available spaces;
- whether booking data can reveal pressure periods during the semester;
- how student data will be protected.

They will emphasise accessibility, privacy, ethical data handling, and support for student experience.

Your team should prepare by thinking of questions you would ask stakeholders to refine the system requirements. Examples include:

- What types of resources should the system manage?
- Who should be allowed to create, update, or delete bookings?
- Should the system show individual-level records or only summary-level reports?
- What booking conflicts should the system detect?
- What reports would be most useful for staff?
- What privacy and ethical issues should be considered?

You should carefully read both the assessment brief and this scenario file.
